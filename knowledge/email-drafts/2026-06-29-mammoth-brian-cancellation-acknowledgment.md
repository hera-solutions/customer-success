## Why this is structured this way

- **Acknowledges directly, no preamble.** Brian's email was warm and final. The reply matches that without buying into the finality.
- **Doesn't accept the loss.** No "sorry to see Mammoth go." That phrasing signals the decision is settled and forecloses the save attempt Matthew typically takes. The current draft holds the door open.
- **Matthew is brought in by name, no title.** Brian knows who he is.
- **"He'll reach out directly before we get into the closure piece"** primes Brian to engage with Matthew personally rather than treating this as a paperwork exchange. It signals there's a real conversation coming.
- **Data retention is answered in full, up front.** Brian asked about it explicitly. The answer is honest about the limitation (no self-serve bulk export) and concrete about the path (email support@hera.app, typically 2 business days for specific items, with realistic examples like associate communications and vehicle details). Answering it completely now frees Matthew to focus entirely on the save conversation rather than splitting attention between salvage and procedural Q&A.
- **No effective date ask.** That's a closure question and assumes the cancellation is final. Removed.
- **No competitor ask.** Matthew will ask "which program are you switching to?" in his own voice, the way he always does. Asking it here would step on his lead.
- **Door is fully open.** The close is one word ("Best,") with no "feel free to" filler. Tone says: respect for Brian's decision, but we're not done yet.
- **No em dashes. No "circle back." No "as soon as possible." No "as per our policy."**
