Part 01

Why we are changing how this works

Zoho has been creating customer success tasks for over a year, using five automated rules that watch a date field called Last_Active1. The idea was reasonable: if a customer has not been active recently, someone should call them.

The problem is what counts as "active." That date refreshes when anything at all happens on the account. A driver opening a notification counts. A background process ticking over counts. So the date almost never goes stale, and when it does, it is often for a customer who is perfectly healthy.

Two things followed from that:

  • It found almost nothing. Eighteen tasks in fourteen months, across a book of 241 customers.
  • What it did find was mostly wrong. On the morning of 08-04-2026 those rules produced six tasks. Not one was for a paying customer who was actually at risk.

Meanwhile the task queue had grown to 217 overdue items that nobody could realistically work through. Those have now been closed and audited, with a record of what was in them.

The lesson we took from it

The old system measured whether anything happened. What we actually need to know is whether a person there is doing the work Hera is for. Those are very different questions, and only the second one predicts anything.

Three things follow from this

We never contact drivers. No calls, no texts, no emails. Every conversation is with a user, and "reached" on a task always means a user.

Anything a driver thinks reaches us second-hand. When you log the blocker Drivers will not use it, that is an owner or dispatcher telling you what a driver told them. Record it word for word and note whose words they are, because we have no way to check it. Driver-side adoption is genuinely unmeasured and it is top of the list of things to instrument.

Drivers drive the invoice and users do not. Users and drivers are separate lists with unrelated counts. Deactivating every user but the owner changes the bill by nothing.

Two places this has already caught us out

"Matthew deactivated all the associates" was wrong. He deactivated users, everyone except the owner. No driver record changed and the invoice was untouched.

And the paperwork signal in Part 03 turned out to be 94% vehicle photos. A driver photographing a van on their pre-trip check and a manager filing a licence were sharing one date. One is a driver action, the other is an office job.

What to call them in front of a customer

Say "drivers". Operators say drivers or associates. They never say "staff" and they certainly never say "users". Say "your team", not "your users".

Part 03

How we tell if a customer is really using Hera

We looked at every customer who left in the last twelve months and asked which activity stopped first. Four things stood out. We call them signs of life, and they are the only four that decide whether an account needs a call.

Sign of lifeWhat literally happenedPredicts leaving
1A message was sent to driversA user sent a message out of Hera to their driversStrongest
2VPL photos came back from driversA driver returned the photos a user asked them for. See belowSecond
3A route was assigned on the daily rosterSomebody was actually put on a route for a given dayThird
4The billed driver count changedThe number of drivers we invoice went up or downFourth

Each of those names the actual operation on purpose. An earlier version said "a schedule was built", which sounds clearer and is worse: it does not tell you whether the customer made an empty roster or genuinely put drivers on routes, and those are different conversations. Every label here is the thing you can say out loud to a customer without being wrong.

Sign 2 in full, because it is the one people get wrong

A Vehicle Photo Log, or VPL, is a request from a user to a driver to photograph a van. It has nothing to do with paperwork. Here is the whole sequence:

  1. A user sends a VPL link to a specific driver, naming the photos they want: front of vehicle, and so on. 90% of VPLs start this way.
  2. The VPL is attached to a rostered day. Every single one, so a customer effectively has to be using the daily roster for VPLs to work at all.
  3. The driver opens the link on their phone and adds the photos, without logging in. This is the whole staff model from Part 02: no account, no password.
  4. Each photo lands in Hera, and that is what the signal reads.

So sign 2 is the only one that proves both halves of the loop are alive: a user asked, and a driver answered. That is why it predicts churn second-strongest of the four. When it goes quiet, either the office stopped asking or the drivers stopped responding, and both are worth a call.

The big weakness in all four signs, and what we are doing about it

All four ask "how long since this last happened", and that is too slow for a customer who falls off a cliff.

Tala Logistics assigned 45 to 56 routes every single day through July, then exactly zero from 29 July. They still have 132 drivers on the books. Seven days later, all four signs still read healthy, because seven days is well inside thirty. We would not have known until late August.

So there is now a second measure that watches the rate against each customer's own normal. It found 19 accounts falling off the roster, 9 of which the four signs currently call healthy, worth $10,259 a month. Two of them, Derby Deliveries and Orad Logistics, have grown their driver count while stopping rostering completely, which means they are dispatching somewhere else.

Always check the driver count next to it. A roster drop with drivers still there is an adoption problem and a call for us. A roster drop where the driver count also collapsed means the business is shrinking, and that is Matthew's, not ours. Five of the nineteen were that, including one that went from 105 drivers to 1.

This is not creating tasks yet. It would take the urgent list from 3 accounts to 17, which is a decision rather than a switch.

Signs 2 and 3 look like the same thing. They are not

VPLs need the daily roster, so you would expect an account with no roster to have no VPLs. The gap is that a daily roster can exist with nobody assigned to it.

22 accounts have VPL photos coming back but have not assigned a route in 30 days, and 80 of their 82 rosters are completely empty. They create the roster, which is enough to make VPLs work, and then never put anyone on a route.

That is two different depths of the same job, which is why both earn a place: the roster existing, and someone actually being put on a route.

The rule: if three of those four have been silent for 30 days or more, the account needs a call.

Three rather than one, because plenty of healthy customers skip one of them permanently. Some never upload paperwork and never will. Judging on a single missing sign would flag a third of the book and you would stop trusting it inside a week.

How we found this out

Sign 2 was originally written down as "driver paperwork uploaded". John asked what that actually included, and it turned out Hera keeps every file in one place, 20 million of them, and the signal just read the newest one. Across the whole book over 90 days: 94% are VPL photos, 5% are coaching evidence, and 0.7% is real paperwork like a licence.

Real paperwork could never be one of the four: 164 of 241 accounts have filed nothing against a person in 30 days, so absence is completely normal and flagging it would flood the queue.

The most useful thing to remember

Getting an account out of trouble takes only one sign coming back. Every measure is "days since it last happened," so the moment somebody there sends a single message, that clock resets to zero and the account drops off the list.

So when you ask a customer to do something, ask for whichever one is easiest for them, not the one you think matters most. One text to their drivers is usually the shortest route back.

Why we do not judge on one number

Worked exampleDBE Logistics

By one measure, DBE looked like one of the fourteen least engaged customers in the entire book. Almost nobody there clicks anything.

They are the opposite of disengaged. They upload Amazon scorecards, and Hera turns those into 9,400 coaching records. One deliberate action a week produces more coaching than most of the book manages in a year.

The reason they looked dead is that our activity log only records things a person clicks. Everything Hera generates on the customer's behalf is invisible to it. If you ever see a task that contradicts what you know about a customer, trust what you know and say so. That is how we found this one.

One honest caveat. These four signs and the 30-day line come from 66 customers who left. That is enough to be useful and not enough to be certain. Expect it to be adjusted once we have a quarter of real call notes, which is exactly what the logging in Part 06 is for.

The thing to notice

Everything stopped in the same week. Their last new driver was added on 06-15. The last person marked as leaving was 06-10. Schedules and paperwork both stopped on 06-14. This is not a gradual decline, it is a cliff, and something specific happened in mid-June.

And the frozen roster is the part that matters most. The number of drivers we bill TPE for changed 132 times in the last year, moving most weeks. On 06-14 it stopped at 115 and has been exactly 115 every single day since. A 115-driver DSP does not stop gaining and losing people for fifty days, so the honest reading is that people have left and nobody has taken them off the list.

Which means we are probably billing them for drivers who no longer work there. We charge per active driver, so a list nobody maintains keeps invoicing. That makes this at least partly a money conversation, and possibly a refund, which is Matthew's. Check the roster before you ask them for anything.

How to open it

You How's the operation running right now, are you still on 115 drivers? I ask because your list hasn't changed since the middle of June, and I want to make sure you're not paying for people who've already moved on.

That opens on their business, states something true you can verify, and puts money back in their pocket as the reason for the call. It is very hard to be annoyed at somebody opening that way.

Then name it once, and stop talking

You The reason I'm calling is that as far as I can see nothing has happened in Hera on your side since about June. No messages, no schedules, nothing. That's usually a sign something changed at your end, and I'd rather ask than guess.

"I'd rather ask than guess" is the whole posture. Then be quiet and let them fill the silence.

Do not ask this

Never ask "are you still using Hera at all?" Put that to a growing customer paying $931 a month and a fair number will say "no, cancel it." You would have talked a healthy account into leaving. An earlier version of our own script said to ask exactly that. It has been removed.

When they do not pick up, and when they do

Owner-operators are hard to reach. That is normal and it is planned for.

If there is silence

Four attempts across different channels, then it leaves you. These are working days, so a Friday trigger means the first attempt is Monday.

Working dayWhat you do
1Call the owner, leave a voicemail
3Text the owner
5Email, and try a second person at the company

Day 5 is the important one, and it is the second contact rather than the change of channel. One of the most common reasons an account goes quiet is that the person who used Hera left the company. No amount of texting will reach them.

Ten working days is about two calendar weeks. Of customers in this situation, none has ever left within the first 30 days of going quiet, so the ladder finishes comfortably inside the window where a call can still work. By day 60, though, 38% are gone. That is the actual deadline.

The moment they say anything, stop the ladder

The schedule above is for silence only. A text back is contact. "Can't talk, try Thursday" is contact. Do not fire attempt three at somebody who already replied. Work the information instead, and what they tell you decides who owns it next, which is often not us.

Part 07

Where you write it down

Seven fields on the task, plus two ordinary ones you also touch. The system fills Next Action and Description; you fill the rest. Across enough calls the pattern in these is worth more than the outcome of any single call, and it is how we find out whether the 30-day rule is right.

The rule that matters more than any single field

An empty field is a fact. A guessed field is a lie that gets averaged into a decision.

If you did not reach the customer, only two fields get filled. If you reached them and they never mentioned something, leave that field alone. A blank is visibly missing and someone can go and get it; a guess looks like data forever.

Start with Contact Outcome. It decides everything else

Every field, one at a time

Contact Outcomepick one · always · fill first

Did you get to a human who can act? A receptionist is not Reached. Neither is a driver who says they will pass it on.

Rescheduled counts as contact and stops the chase. So does a text back. Do not keep working the calendar at somebody who already answered.

There is no "wrong number" option. If the line is dead, use No answer and put the detail in Ask Made.

Why it matters: this is the only field the old automation never fills, so it is how we count who actually got contacted. Blank means the call never happened as far as any report is concerned, even if you made it.

Next Actionpick one · always · pre-set for you

This field is the ladder position, and there is no counter anywhere else. If you do not move it, the next person cannot tell which attempt they are on.

It arrives at 1 Call and voicemail for an urgent task, or Single outreach for a quarterly one. Advance it as you go: text, then email plus a second person, then a final attempt, then Escalate to Matthew.

Stop advancing it the moment you reach someone. Set what genuinely happens next, or leave it and close the task.

Job Namedpick one · only if Reached

The part of their day they told you was hardest. Not the feature they are missing, and not what you think their problem is.

Six choices matching the six jobs, plus two that matter more than the six: Wants only part of it and Hera does not do it.

Those last two are not failures. "Wants only part of it" is a pricing conversation for Matthew. "Hera does not do it" is product feedback and is worth more to the company than saving the account.

If they never named a hardest part, leave it empty. Do not infer it from whichever feature went quiet.

Blockerpick one · only if Reached · most valuable field here

Why they stopped, in their explanation rather than yours. Six options, all of which came out of real churn analysis: nobody was shown how, the person who did it left, it broke or was slow, they do it elsewhere now, they forgot it existed, the drivers will not use it.

One account's blocker is an anecdote. Forty accounts' blockers are a roadmap, which is the whole reason this field exists.

Do not reach for "Other" to avoid choosing. If you do use it, writing the reason in the Description is not optional.

One ask per call. Two is a to-do list and a to-do list gets ignored. Getting an account off the urgent list takes only one sign of life coming back, so ask for whichever is easiest for them. Usually that is a single message to their drivers.

Where to actually find these fields

They are in the full task record, not the popup. When you click a task from a list, Zoho shows a compact card with six fields: Subject, Priority, Due Date, Status, Related To, Owner. None of the seven are on it. Open the record itself, and keep scrolling: the last two sit below Customer Quote, which is a tall free-text box.

Flagging this because John went looking for two of them in the popup and concluded they had never been built. If you cannot find a field, it is almost certainly one level deeper rather than missing.

Hard rule, no exceptions

Nothing automated will ever write, edit or delete a Note. Ever. Notes are the human record, written by you three, and you already have a convention for them. The nine fields above are the structured record. Two records, two jobs, no overlap. If a script started writing notes alongside people, nobody could trust the customer history again.

Nobody marks their own homework

There is no "did it work?" box, deliberately. The next morning's run is the answer. If the account has come back to life, it worked, or they recovered on their own, and either way it drops off your list. If it is still quiet and you reached them, the ask did not stick and you need a different one, not the same one again. If it is still quiet and you never reached them, it counts toward the handover on day 10.

Part 09

Things we never do

  • Never offer money off because someone is not using the product. This has already cost us. One account was given a 100% credit, roughly $1,040 a month, labelled "not using it," and no adoption conversation was ever had. That turned a fixable problem into permanent lost revenue and hid it from our own data. Any pricing question goes to Matthew and you say you will come back to them.
  • Never open with "we noticed you're not using..." It is an accusation about something they pay for, and they will spend the rest of the call defending themselves.
  • Never mention renewal. There is no renewal at Hera. Month to month, no contract, no term. Implying one is not true.
  • Never invent a time-saved figure. Do not multiply anything by assumed minutes. If a customer says "this saves me about six hours a week," that is quotable and we will use it with their name on it. Anything we calculate ourselves is not.
  • Never discuss billing with an AMP account. They do not have an invoice.
  • Never write, edit or delete a Note.
