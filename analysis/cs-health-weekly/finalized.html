The model

Engagement does not predict revenue decline. This was tested, not assumed. In a 20-account pilot, 6 of 15 declining accounts scored above the healthy average on engagement, the single most engaged account was losing 15% of its associates, and the least engaged healthy account was growing 20%. A blended score hides both failure modes.

  • Engagement answers will they stay at all. Human-sent messages, message reads, scorecard uploads, and staffed rosters.
  • Revenue direction answers how much will they pay. Active-associate trend across the last four closed invoices.

What was built

  • analysis/tenant-engagement/lib_hera.py — read-only session, table names, and every field trap centralised.
  • analysis/tenant-engagement/three_signals.py — pulls all signals. 252 tenants, zero errors, about $0.03 a run.
  • analysis/tenant-engagement/classify.py — two axes to one bucket. Reads the cached pull, so threshold changes re-run instantly with no API calls.
  • cs-health-weekly skill — the authoritative weekly report.
  • Daily review Part 4 reduced to a tripwire that defers to the weekly scorer instead of classifying.
  • analysis/cs-health-baseline-2026-07/findings.md — the full baseline and every rejected alternative.

The daily review previously specified a three-signal model but the scripts it referenced were never in the repo, so classification was re-derived by hand on every run. Those paths now resolve.

Round two, 30 July: five measurement bugs

BugEffectFix
Message search swamped by automated sendsRoster and coaching messages are delivered per associate and carry no sender. A tenant with 100 associates emits 100+ sender-less rows a minute, pushing the human-sent message out of any fixed page. 59 of 252 tenants read as darker than they were, 14 by a week or more. One read as 1,205 days dark while messaging that same day.Search back by time across all types, stop at the first human send, and flag truncation rather than silently reporting dark.
Roster scan consumed by future planningTenants plan weeks ahead, so their newest rosters are future-dated shells with no routes yet. The scan cap ran out before reaching real work. 23 tenants showed no staffed roster; 6 had staffed one within three days, three of them that same day.Only consider rosters dated today or earlier, and raise the cap.
Uncollected revenue counted as ARROnly Pending was excluded, so Written Off and Payment Error invoices counted as collected.Revenue comes from Paid invoices only. Delinquency is now its own flag.
Twelve-month churn understated by halfEach account's final invoice is often a partial stub for the few days before cancellation, then written off. 25 of 88 accounts had one. One showed $6/mo instead of $1,133/mo. The twelve-month total was $27,360/mo against a true $65,098/mo.Use the last full month, skipping any final month under 60% of the prior norm.
A negative invoice subtracting from ARRA $10 fixed discount against a $9 charge for one associate invoiced -$1.00. A genuine billing defect, trivial in value.Clamp to zero for ARR and flag the credit.

Still open

  1. Seasonality. Every threshold comes from a mid-summer window, so a July decline cannot yet be separated from a seasonal trough. This is the one input that could move numbers.
  2. Routing and ownership per bucket. Adoption risk plausibly belongs with CSS 1 as an adoption conversation rather than with John as a save.
  3. Response times. Now answerable, since the queues are small.
  4. Billing anomalies. Philosophe LLC and Integrated Logistics bill $0 with associates and no discount label; Crucial Mile has no premium status recorded. About $17,000 a year, pending the conversation with Matthew.
  5. Trials. 67 real trials hold $722,952 of potential and nobody is working either the engaged or the unengaged half.
  6. No outcome log. Nothing records what intervention was tried or whether it worked, so no threshold here has been calibrated against a real result.
