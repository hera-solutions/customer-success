How our money works3 / 13

We get paid when our customers hire drivers

We charge $9 per active driver per month, worked out daily at 30 cents a head, and we invoice a month behind. The number of drivers explains 96% of what any customer pays us.

Our revenue is essentially a headcount tax. If a customer hires 20 more drivers we automatically collect $180 more next month without doing anything. If they lose 20 drivers we lose $180. That is why revenue went up this year with nobody selling: our customers hired.

The flip side is that we have almost no way to charge an existing customer more. The price per driver is fixed and only 4 of our 248 customers pay any kind of flat fee. The one real upsell we own is HeraAi, which is switched on for 13% of customers.

27 customers worth $24,245 a month are "not using it." These are the strange ones. They are hiring more drivers, so our invoice to them goes up every month, and yet they barely touch the product. One is up 25% on drivers and has not built a driver schedule in 83 days.

35 customers worth $28,268 a month have "stopped scorecards." They still message their drivers through Hera, but they have not uploaded an Amazon performance scorecard in over two months. They are doing that part of the job somewhere else now, which means half our product has already been replaced for them.

5 customers worth $3,832 a month are critical: losing drivers and not using the product. That is small enough to work through this week.

Two other groups matter for the opposite reason: leave them alone.

31 customers are shrinking but fully engaged. Their own businesses are getting smaller. That is not a failure on our side and chasing them wastes the time we need for the other groups.

The opportunity nobody is working9 / 13

Our free trials are worth more than everything we are worried about

We have 67 companies sitting in a free trial right now, holding 6,648 drivers between them. If they all converted at our normal price they would be worth $59,832 a month, which is more than the entire $52,512 we are worried about losing.

Nobody owns this. It is not on my list and it is not on either CSS's list. 35 of them are actively scheduling drivers and messaging, so they are live chances to convert, worth $33,561 a month. The other 32 have gone quiet or never really started, worth $26,271 a month. One of those has 287 drivers and has never once built a driver schedule.

An average customer is worth about $800 a month. So converting one trial is worth roughly the same as saving one at-risk customer, and there are 67 of them sitting untouched.

A different and more urgent problem I found while re-checking the data: we are delivering and not being paid.

JDW Logistics, our largest customer at 338 drivers, has had its last three invoices written off. April, May and June, about $2,883 a month. It is a healthy, growing, active customer using the product every day. PacTrack has had two card payments fail in a row, and Pure Logistics one.

Together that is $4,449 a month we are supplying for free without deciding to.

What I need from you12 / 13

Five decisions

  1. Replace the retention target. We are already keeping nearly everything we can, so the old target tells us nothing. I suggest we set it higher, or move the goal onto trials where there is real room to improve.
  2. Sort out the three unexplained billing accounts. About $1,400 a month. I do not want to touch them until I know whether somebody made those calls deliberately.
  3. Confirm the seven other free accounts. MBB I understand and agree with. The rest I cannot account for.
  4. Decide whether we work the trials. $59,832 a month sitting there and currently nobody's job. If yes it needs an owner, and it competes directly with the 67 at-risk customers.
  5. Decide about the old pricing. Nine customers still pay $5 to $7 a driver instead of $9. Moving them up is fair, but "too expensive" already cost us $6,614 a month this year, so we should sequence it carefully rather than send one email.

What happens anyway, and how much to trust this13 / 13

The work that needs no decision, and the two things I am not sure about

Already running or ready to go: the weekly customer check takes minutes and costs about three cents. Calling the 5 critical customers this week. Starting adoption conversations with the largest "not using it" customers, framed as value rather than rescue. Asking the 35 scorecard customers what happened rather than assuming. Chasing the unpaid invoices starting with JDW. None of this needs budget or a hire.

What I am confident about. Every figure here comes from our own production data and has been checked twice. I found and fixed five measurement mistakes on 30 July, including one that made 59 customers look more abandoned than they were and one that understated our losses by half. Eighteen consistency checks now pass and the revenue figures match our invoices to the dollar.

What I am not confident about. First, seasonality. Every cut-off point I use comes from one summer snapshot, so a drop in July might just be what July looks like here.

Second, we have never recorded what works. Nothing anywhere tracks what we tried on a customer and whether it helped. So these warning signs are informed judgement, not proven predictions. Six months of writing down what we did and what happened turns this from a good guess into something we can rely on.
