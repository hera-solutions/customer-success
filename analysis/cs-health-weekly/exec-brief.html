How to read this deck

Every dollar figure is monthly revenue, not yearly. We bill month to month with no contract, so a customer can leave at the end of any month. Talking in yearly figures would pretend we have a commitment we do not have.

So when you see $24,245, read it as "that is how much money arrives every month from that group of customers." Multiply by twelve in your head only if you want the yearly picture.

How our money works3 / 14

We get paid when our customers hire drivers

We charge $9 per active associate per month, worked out daily at 30 cents a head, and we invoice a month behind. The number of active associates explains 96% of what any customer pays.

$801Average customerPer month.
$759Middle customerHalf pay more, half pay less.

In plain terms

Our revenue is basically a headcount tax. If a customer hires 20 more drivers, we automatically get $180 more a month without doing anything. If they lose 20, we lose $180.

Two consequences. First, we grow when our customers grow, which is why revenue went up without any selling. Second, we have almost no way to charge an existing customer more, because the price per head is fixed and only 4 of our 248 customers pay any kind of flat fee.

The only lever we have is selling something that is not priced per head. HeraAi is on 13% of accounts. That is the one real upsell we own.

In plain terms

We sorted every customer into one of nine groups. Two of them matter most and neither is about customers leaving.

"Not using it" is the strange one: these customers are growing, so our invoice to them goes up every month, and yet they barely touch the product. They are not angry, they are not complaining, they simply are not getting value. The first time anyone in their office asks what the $1,750 is for, we lose them.

"Stopped scorecards" means they still use Hera to message drivers but have moved the performance side of their job somewhere else. That is a warning that half our product has already been replaced.

Together that is $52,512 a month, and until now nobody knew who those 62 customers were.

In plain terms

Both of these would look like customers in trouble on a simpler report, and both would be wrong.

The first group has already effectively left; we just have not seen the final invoice yet. Calling that money "recoverable" would make our risk number look bigger and our save rate look worse than reality.

The second group did exactly what we asked them to do. We remind customers twice a month to remove drivers who left. When they do, their bill goes down. That is good hygiene, not churn, and running a save call on them would be confusing.

I am flagging both because being able to say which numbers are not scary is what makes the scary ones believable.

How much to trust these numbers14 / 14

What I am confident about, and what I am not

Confident. Every figure here comes from our own production data, checked twice. I found and fixed five measurement mistakes on 30 July, including one that made 59 customers look more abandoned than they were and one that understated our yearly losses by half. Eighteen consistency checks now pass, and the revenue figures reconcile to the cent against our invoices.

Not confident. Two things.

Seasonality. All my cut-off points come from one summer snapshot. A drop in July might just be what July looks like.

We have never recorded what works. Nothing anywhere tracks what we tried on a customer and whether it helped. So these warning signs are informed judgement, not proven predictions. Six months of writing down what we did and what happened turns this from a good guess into something we can rely on.
